## 1. Welcome aboard

You are the new Captain of the **DSV Aqua Nova**, a deep-submergence research vessel run by **O.E.R.A.** (the Oceanic Exploration and Research Alliance). Admiral Catherine Okafor has brought you out of retirement to commission her: to take her through her trials and prove her ready.

### Your first hour
1. **Sign in.** You arrive in the launch bay and sign in on a crew PED.
2. **Go to your quarters.** Walk through the walkways. The way to your quarters glows.
3. **Read the logbook.** The laptop on the desk glows. Admiral Okafor's welcome letter is the first entry.
4. **Collect your PED** (on the bed) and your **communicator pin** (on the desk).
5. **Call ARIA.** Tap the pin. ARIA, the ship's artificial intelligence and your Executive Officer, takes you through the ship.
6. **Go to the bridge.** ARIA introduces the bridge crew. Your commissioning orders arrive by mail soon after.

### Rooms open with the story
At first you can walk to the launch bay, the two walkways, your quarters and the bridge. Other rooms open when a mission needs them: the **science lab and sick bay** with your first survey, **engineering** when you reach the trials area. A locked door gets an excuse from ARIA and a reminder of your current task.

### Tasks
Your current task is always in **PED → Tasks**. One step shows at a time, with how many are still to come.

## 2. The ship

| | |
|---|---|
| Type | Deep-submergence research vessel (DSV) |
| Power | Twin **quantum fusion** plant, fuel taken from sea water |
| Drive | Two **hydro-jets** (port and starboard), plus the fusion drive for super cruise |
| Speed | **14 kt** submerged on the jets; **7 kt** on the surface; **100 kt** in super cruise |
| Depth | Crush depth **11,000 m**; the charts go to 6,000 m |
| Craft | Four **WSKRs**, the science ROV **Remora**, the shuttle **Tern** |

### Decks and rooms
- **Walkway A1:** the straight corridor. **Bridge** at the end, **Science** (near left), **Medical** (far left), **Quarters** (near right).
- **Walkway AN-1:** the hub. **Launch Bay** (the left end door), **Engineering** (near right).
- Tap the floor at the bottom of a walkway to walk to the other walkway.

### Power and battery
The fusion plant never needs fuel. The **battery** is the ship's reserve for everything else (the "hotel load"): lights, sonar, the sweep, the cabin. It drains faster at speed and much faster in super cruise. Docked, shore power charges it.

## 3. The crew and the communicator

| Crew member | Job | Where you meet them |
|---|---|---|
| **ARIA** | Executive Officer, the ship's AI | Everywhere (the communicator) |
| **Marcus Chen** | Helm | The bridge, centre seat |
| **Nicole Brooks** | Communications | The bridge, left console |
| **Alex Torres** | Sensors (sonar, WSKRs) | The bridge, right console |
| **Dr. Samira Patel** | Science and the sick bay | The science lab |
| **Chief Ryan Doyle** | Chief Engineer | Engineering |
| **Lt. Andre Washington** | Security; runs the launch bay and the Tern | The launch bay |

### The communicator
Tap the **pin** to open it. Pick a crew member to talk. Each has:
- **Ask about …** background topics, and how-to guides for their screens ("Buttons on my pages" and tasks).
- **Orders …** what they can do for you now. An order shows only when it makes sense.
- **EMERGENCY …** real emergency actions (only for crew who have them).
- **Call another station** and **That's all**.

You can reach a crew member only after you have met them in their room. Until then, ARIA passes on what they say ("Engineering reports: …").

### Messages
- **Important messages open the conversation** with that crew member. If you are reading, a new line waits behind **Continue**; nothing is lost.
- **Short replies** (to a button you pressed) and routine reports do not open a conversation. On the bridge they show on the **overhead panel**; elsewhere they go into that crew member's history and the **pin pulses**.
- Anything longer than a short line opens the conversation instead, so nothing important is squeezed onto the panel.
- **Warnings on the panel:** red for damage and errors (it flashes), amber for cautions.

### Who tells you what
- **ARIA:** hints (after a minute or so), locked doors, mission congratulations, the general alarm.
- **Marcus:** the helm, docking, terrain warnings, the Tern's mission updates.
- **Nicole:** mail, reports, the surface link lost and back, the Tern by radio.
- **Alex:** contacts (hostiles too), WSKRs, the sonar.
- **Dr. Patel:** Remora launches and recoveries, samples, analysis.
- **Chief Doyle:** engineering, damage, the trials' depth pushes.
- **Lt. Washington:** the Tern's launches and recoveries, a parted Remora tether.

## 4. The bridge

### The main screen
Shows the **nav chart** (or the page you put there with DSP), with the speed, depth, floor and heading on the left and the **vertical profile** on the right (the sea floor ahead, with yellow and red warning lines).

### The overhead panel
The status line: your place (or the next waypoint), position, date and time. **Notices** from the crew show here one at a time, each held long enough to read (5 to 12 seconds); the next waits its turn.

### The desk
Three pads, **five buttons each**:
- **Left pad: the page.** NAV, HLM, TAC, SNR, VW, SYS. The page shows on the centre display.
- **Centre pad: what the page does.**
- **Right pad: more of the same page.**

| Page | Centre pad | Right pad |
|---|---|---|
| **NAV** | Dest, Route, Plot | PAN, SHW |
| **HLM** | Port, Stbd, Stop, Slow, Fast | Up, Dn, Srfc, NOE, Cruise |
| **TAC** | (not installed) | |
| **SNR** | Source, Active, Full sweep, Scan site, Survey site | Scan, Probe, Clear |
| **VW** | Lights, camera or synthetic view | |
| **SYS** | status, Lights, Sonar | |

Also on the desk:
- **Two yellow keys** (left of the centre display): **+** zooms in, **−** zooms out (one range for every page).
- **The round trackpad** (right of the centre display): **DSP**, puts the selected page on the main screen. Its lamp is lit while on.

### The bridge crew
Tap **Marcus**, **Nicole** or **Alex** to talk. Tap Marcus's left monitor for the Helm station; Nicole's console for Comms; Alex's for Sensors. His two small monitors show heading and speed, and depth and floor.

## 5. Helm and navigation

### Course and speed are separate
- **Plotting a course steers.** It does not change the speed. Set a speed at the Helm too.
- **Changing speed keeps the course.**
- **Steering by hand cancels the course.**
- On arrival the autopilot stops the ship.

### Making a destination (NAV → Dest)
Three ways: **Place** (pick a charted place), **Lat / Long** (degrees and minutes: 39 54.00 N is 39°54′, not 39.54°), or **Bearing / Distance** from a place. The dialog reads it back so you can check.

### The route
**Route** lists the waypoints. Tap a row, then **+** adds one after it, **−** deletes it, **▲ ▼** move it, **Edit** changes it. **Done** keeps it, **Cancel** drops it. **Plot** has the helm plan a path round the land.

### Speed orders
Tell Marcus **Make your speed**: All stop, 5, 8 (bay safe), 10, or Full (14 knots). Once the Sea Trials have run the jets past their rating, a **Max tested** option appears (15.5 or 17 knots): the jets run past their rating until your next speed order.

### Depth
- **Dive rate depends on speed**, like a plane: about 0.5 m/s stopped, 2.6 m/s at 14 kt, up to 3 m/s (10 m/s in super cruise).
- **Periscope depth:** 10 m.
- **Surface:** you are limited to 7 kt.
- **Terrain protection:** if a depth order would take the ship into the sea floor within about a minute of travel, Marcus holds 10 m above it, and says so. **Your order stands:** when the floor drops away he carries on down to the depth you asked for.
- **Hug the floor (NOE):** the helm keeps the ship 10 m above the bottom, looking ahead. Needs 30 m of water. Any depth order ends it.

### Super cruise
- The fusion drive takes her to **100 kt**. It **spools up for 8 seconds**, then eases up to speed (and eases down after).
- Needs: undocked, power, and at least **120 m of water** under the ship and ahead. If the water ahead gets shallow, she drops out on her own.
- **Any other power setting** (Slow, Fast, Stop, a speed order) drops super cruise.
- At speed the ship is noisy: she hears less (the sonar's range falls to 40% at 100 kt).

### Docking
At a dock, under 1.5 kt and at 5 m or shallower: **Dock** (Helm station). Marcus says one line; you hear the fenders, four clamps and the shore power. Docked, the battery charges, the air refills, fresh scrubbers come aboard and the hull is repaired. **Undock** before you leave.

### Charts
- **Colours:** bold dark yellow is **land**; light yellow is **shallow water** (20 m or less); blue lines are depth contours.
- **Fog of war:** the chart starts rough. Wherever the ship (or a WSKR) has been, the sea floor stays charted in detail. The ship maps **4 nm** round her all the time, with her survey sensors (not the sonar).
- **Area of Operations:** outside the charted area is hatched red. Missions may add a box (a trials or patrol area).
- **Zoom:** 0.5 to 256 nm.

## 6. Sonar and the sensors

### Sonar modes
| Mode | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Off** | The ship hears only about 1 nm. Contacts fade away. |
| **Passive** | Listening. The normal setting. |
| **Active** | A ping every 20 seconds. Each ping spreads out like a ripple (1.5 nm a second, drawn on the sonar screen and the charts). Once the first ripple has passed a ship, she is heard **twice as far**. |

**The cost of going active:** everyone hears the ping. **Poachers within 24 nm run.** **Other hostile ships within 36 nm turn toward you.** Alex says so when he can see it.

Switch: the **Active** button (SNR page, Sensors station), or tell Alex **Go active / Go passive / Sonar off**.

### How hearing works
- Hearing is a **chance**, not a wall. Each ship has a range where you hear it half the time: about **12 nm** for the ship's passive sonar (6 nm for a WSKR), larger for big or fast ships, smaller for small or slow ones, and less when you are fast (super cruise).
- A ship at the edge fades in and out.
- **A name belongs to a track.** Lose a ship for two minutes and her name is lost too; picked up again, she is a "probable match" until identified again.

### The contact list
Each contact shows a bearing line. **Unknown contacts** are yellow. Once identified the colour shows the side: **blue** friendly, **green** neutral, **orange** unfriendly, **red** hostile.

### Getting a fix
One bearing gives a direction. **Two bearings from different places** (the ship and a WSKR, at 20° or more apart) give a position, drawn with an error ring.

### Identifying a ship
- **AIS:** commercial and friendly ships say who they are (VHF mast up).
- Any ship **within 4 nm** is identified.
- A **WSKR within 2 nm** identifies her.
- **Scan** (with a contact picked): within 6 nm, or 12 nm if her signature is on file. About 10 seconds.
- **Hail** her on VHF.

### Buttons on the sonar page
- **Source:** listen with the ship or a WSKR.
- **Active:** see above.
- **Full sweep:** a **mapping sweep** with the ship's survey sensors. It turns once round (about 40 seconds), fills in the chart to **12 nm**, and **draws a lot of power**. It does not help hear ships.
- **Scan site** (at a site you have not scanned): file a scan.
- **Survey site** (at a site): hold station within 0.5 nm at 2 kt or less for **30 minutes**. The sea floor is charted in detail for 3 nm round it and a survey finding is filed.
- **Scan, Probe, Clear** (a contact picked): Scan identifies; **Probe** sends a WSKR to look; Clear deselects.

## 7. WSKRs

Four small autonomous sensor craft: **Scout** (charts a little further), **Lantern** (holds the lead), **Drifter** (swings wide), **Sprat** (faster). Each has its own launch tube.

| | |
|---|---|
| Speed | 16 kt (Sprat 19); 120 kt super speed when far from where they must go |
| Battery | 6 h to start (12 h, then 24 h with research). Recalled by itself at 15%. At 0% it is adrift until picked up |
| Charge | In its tube: full in 2 hours. Needs 20% to launch |
| Link | **10 nm** from the ship or a relay WSKR |

### Launching and recovering
Each WSKR launches from its own tube in **one minute**, below **8 kt**. A recalled WSKR comes home, waits alongside, and is taken aboard when the ship is slow enough.

### Orders (WSKR page, or tell Alex)
**Deploy, Recall all, Hold formation, Search pattern, Pattern… (planner), Set up a relay**, formation **Loose/Tight**, depth **Auto/Shallow/Deep**. For one WSKR: Hold formation, Manual control, Search pattern, Relay, Investigate (the selected contact), Recall.

### The screen
WSKRs ride round the ship as a listening screen. With auto-intercept on, a free WSKR closes on an unidentified ship within 6 nm to identify her, then comes back.

### Out of link range
A WSKR keeps its order. After 10 minutes without contact it surfaces and calls in over the surface link; it dives again when back in range.

### Relay
A relay WSKR rides at the surface near the ship and **gives the ship its surface link and a GPS fix**, at any depth, **as long as it is within 10 nm**.

## 8. Communications

### The links
| Link | Antenna | Works to | Sends? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SAT-COM** (satellite) | Comm buoy | the buoy within 15 m of the surface | Yes |
| **LASER** (satellite) | Mast | 15 m | Yes |
| **HF** (home station) | Mast | 5 m | Yes |
| **VLF** (Cutler, Maine) | VLF wire | 40 m | Receive only |
| **LPI** (acoustic) | Hull transducer | Any depth | Yes, but only to undersea places |

### Antennas
Raise them on **Comms → Controls**. The **mast** comes in above 8 kt, the **comm buoy** above 4 kt (it floats up to 40 m above the ship), the **VLF wire** above 6 kt.

### Staying in touch when deep
- **Float the comm buoy**, **put a WSKR up as a relay**, or **put the Tern up as a relay** (both within 10 nm).
- **Nicole tells you** when the ship loses her last surface link: her message opens, with what to do. If the link comes back before you read it, she drops it. When the link is back she gives a short notice. (Each waits 10 seconds, so a flickering link does not chatter.)

### LPI: the quiet undersea link
A spread-spectrum signal **below the ocean's own noise**. It works at any depth, needs no antenna, and **nobody hears it** (unlike active sonar, it does not give you away). It reaches **undersea colonies and mines**: about **25 nm**, or about **120 nm in the deep sound channel** (600 to 1200 m down). It does not reach the surface network.

### Hailing
**Comms → Contacts.** Pick a band (VHF, HF, UHF, LPI), tune to a contact (or **Scan band**), tap **Hail**. VHF needs the mast up. Meridian Habitat answers on VHF 156.825 MHz and LPI 13.3 kHz.

### Mail and reports
- **Mail** (Comms → Mail): orders, offers and replies from OERA. Nicole tells you when a message comes in; if you read it first, she has nothing to add.
- **Transmit report** (or tell Nicole): uploads on the best link. A progress bar shows on the Comms console. If the link drops it holds; after 40 seconds without a link the report fails and you can send it again. Uploads take about 7 s (laser), 18 s (satellite), 45 s (HF).

## 9. The launch bay, Remora and the Tern

### The main bay
Shared by **Remora** and the **Tern**, one craft at a time. (WSKRs have their own tubes.)
- A launch or recovery takes **one minute**. The bay floods (launch) or is pumped out (recovery); the hatches are quick, the water takes most of the time.
- **Speed:** keep under **8 kt** with the outer doors open. Above 8 kt the hull takes damage; above 10 kt the **doors** are damaged for good (slower launches; at 100% they will not open). Marcus holds you at 8 kt and says so; you can override.
- Not while docked.

### Remora (Dr. Patel)
A small unmanned sample collector on a **tether**.
- **Tether:** 1 nm (1.5 nm with research). **If the ship drags it past its length, it parts**: Remora is lost where she stops, until the Tern fetches her. A new tether takes Engineering 2 hours.
- **Tools:** sample collector (water anywhere; biological and sediment within 6 m of the floor; 5 min), core drill (rock cores near the floor; 15 min), magnetometer (readings at wrecks; 3 min, sent up the tether at once).
- **Storage:** 3 samples.
- **Driving:** set speed, heading and depth with taps, or **Go to** a site; she does 2 kt and dives at 2 m/s. **Auto return** brings her alongside.
- **Recover** (within 0.2 nm, the ship at 8 kt or less). The team unload her **one sample per 2 minutes** (each reaches the lab when unloaded), then re-equip her in 5 minutes.
- While she is out her tether **holds the bay doors open**, so no other craft can use the bay.

### The Tern (Lt. Washington, then Nicole by radio)
A shuttle for crew, supplies, away teams and rescues.

| | |
|---|---|
| Speed | 20 kt (7 kt on the surface) |
| Seats | 2 pilots and 6 passengers (one pilot needs a radio link within 20 nm) |
| Battery | 1 h (3 h, then 6 h with research). Full charge in 2 h in the bay or at a dock |
| Air | 7 h with all 8 aboard (9 h with research); longer with fewer. Refills in 1 h |
| Safe depth | 800 m (crush 1,000 m) |

- **Launch** (tell Lt. Washington): a supply run, search and rescue, or go to a place. Not below 800 m.
- **Once she is out**, Nicole gives her orders by radio: hold, surface, relay, go to, make depth, heading or speed, search pattern, call back.
- If the ship goes below 800 m while she is out, she holds off at a safe depth until you come up.
- **Rescues:** surfaced and within 0.2 nm of a casualty for a minute (or at a sunken sub's depth, within 0.12 nm, for 90 s). Four board at a time. Survivors land in the bay or at a friendly dock.
- She **cannot resupply at an enemy port**.

## 10. The science lab

### Samples and analysis
Samples come from Remora. Each instrument runs one job at a time:

| Instrument | Samples | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Material Analysis** | sediment, rock core | 20 min |
| **Spectral Analysis** | water, magnetic reading | 15 min |
| **Gene Sequencing** | biological | 30 min |

Each has a live display. A finished analysis files a **finding** (PED → Findings) and earns **research points**.

### Research
Spend points on **Research** (lab, research computer):

| Topic | Cost | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Longer tether | 2 | Remora works 1.5 nm out |
| WSKR formations and orders | 4 | More WSKR orders |
| WSKR long-life cells | 3 | WSKR battery 12 h |
| WSKR deep-cycle cells | 5 | WSKR battery 24 h (needs long-life) |
| Tern long-life cells | 3 | Tern battery 3 h |
| Tern deep-cycle cells | 5 | Tern battery 6 h (needs long-life) |
| Tern air scrubbers | 3 | Tern air 9 h |
| Synthetic forward view | 5 | A forward view built from the chart (VW page) |

(Deep sonar sweep, 3 points, has no effect yet.)

## 11. Engineering

The **engineering computer** (the console in Engineering) shows ten systems, each with a health (below 50 degraded, 0 offline), on four power relays. **An untouched ship runs exactly as normal**; the systems matter when you switch something off or something is damaged.

| System | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Power relays** | A propulsion, B life support, C sensors and comms, D hotel |
| **Propulsion** | Two hydro-jets (one out: half speed) and the fusion drive (out: no super cruise) |
| **CO2 scrubbers** | Normal, boost (clears CO2 fast, double use), off |
| **O2** | Electrolysis generator and a reserve tank |
| **Water** | Fresh-water tank and desalinator |
| **Heating/cooling** | Holds the cabin at the set temperature |
| **Fire suppression** | Armed: a fire is out in 20 s. Disarmed: discharge by hand |
| **Bulkheads** | Forward and aft watertight doors, and bilge pumps |
| **Ballast** | Follows the dive; **EMERGENCY BLOW** drives her to the surface |
| **Air charge** | The high-pressure air bank; recharges near the surface. A blow uses 60% |

Each system's page is a **live flow diagram**: tap pumps, valves and breakers to switch them.

### Damage
- Every **impact** damages the hull and a system.
- A hard hit (about 9 kt or faster) starts a **fire**; harder (about 12 kt) opens a **leak**. Close the bulkhead to contain a leak (the crew close it after a minute if you do not).
- **Repairs:** two damage-control teams; tap a system, then REPAIR (25 health an hour, 100 docked). The dock mends the hull.

### General alarm
Sound it (ARIA's emergencies, or the button on the engineering computer) and the ship goes to red lighting. If it is already sounding when a fire or leak starts, the crew are ready: fire out in 8 s, a bulkhead closed in 15 s.

## 12. Air and life support

- **CO2 scrubbers** are single-use canisters, enough for about **90 days** submerged. They wear only below 3 m. While they last, oxygen holds.
- **At the surface** the air is refreshed (about 30% an hour).
- **Docked:** oxygen refills and fresh scrubbers come aboard. A supply run with the Tern restocks them too.
- The O2 generator, the scrubbers, water and heating can all be switched or damaged (chapter 11).

## 13. Missions, OERA and the factions

### Commissioning (the opening missions)
1. **First Contact:** find the pin, call ARIA.
2. **Know Your Ship:** the logbook, the PED, the walkways, the bridge.
3. **Sea Trials:** sail to the Trials AO (about 100 nm south) under 15 kt and above 300 m. Then test depth (300 m), full speed, super cruise, and report. Department heads may ask to **push** each test further (450 then 600 m; 15.5 then 17 kt; 110 then 120 kt) for a ship record, with a risk of light damage. Whoever does it tells you when he starts, what he is waiting for (for example, out of super cruise for the jets-only run), and when he has made it.
4. **Charting a Course:** on the way home: plot to Vineyard Ledge, launch a WSKR, run a sweep, scan the ledge.
5. **First Survey:** Remora samples, analysis, report.

Each step **commissions** a system. ARIA congratulates you when a mission is complete.

### OERA's missions
After the first survey OERA sends **offers** by mail: unknown contacts, site surveys, seabed surveys, distress calls (Tern rescues), and patrols.
- **Accept or Decline.** Declining an optional offer costs nothing; a compulsory one costs standing. **Quit** costs a little.
- **Positions are given, not plotted for you:** lat/long, or bearing and distance from a known place. Plot them yourself.
- **There are no failed missions, only different outcomes.** A peaceful outcome is preferred.
- **Request new orders** on Comms (Orders) when you have none.

### The factions
- **Friendly:** O.E.R.A., the Civil Harbour Authority, the Meridian Free Colony.
- **Neutral:** freight, fishing, yachts, Sable Offshore Energy, the Deepcut and Abyssal Metals mining consortia.
- **Wary:** Concord Naval Command, Ridgeline Extraction.
- **Hostile:** the Grey Net Fleet (poachers), the Black Tide Syndicate (pirates).

What you do changes your **standing** with each (Logbook → Standings).

## 14. Time, sleep and saving

### Time
The game runs in **real time** by default. **PED → Settings → Time compression:** 5x to 120x. Handling (turns, dives) stays in real seconds at any setting.

### Sleep (your quarters)
- **Skip time:** 2, 4, 8, 12 or 24 hours, or until arrival. The crew wake you for an emergency.
- **Crew has the con:** choose a limit (4 to 24 hours), sleep, and **close the app**. When you come back the game catches up the real time that passed, and ARIA briefs you. The crew keep your course and speed, stop at the area limit, on arrival, on a shoal, or when air, battery or hull run low, and may send an away team for supplies or Remora for samples.

### Saving
- The game saves when you leave, and at every logbook entry.
- **Logbook:** you can go back to any entry, or **Restart from the beginning** from the first one.

## 15. The PED

Your personal tablet (top corner button):
- **Status:** the ship at a glance.
- **Missions:** active, available and completed.
- **Tasks:** the current step of each mission (and your own tasks).
- **Contacts:** the crew you have met, with their bios.
- **Findings:** survey and science results.
- **Journal:** your notes, and arrival notes.
- **Settings:** sound (a mixer, the theme, the **sound board**), time compression, text size, display pictures (smooth or retro), sync with the iPhone PED, game version, bug notes.

---

### The iPhone PED
A companion app for your phone (the same PED, portrait). It shows what the iPad last reported: status, an **Alerts** card (alarms, fire, flooding, damage, no surface link, active sonar), a nav chart, missions, findings, and your tasks and journal (these two sync both ways). Connect it in its Settings with the same GitHub token and passphrase as the iPad (PED → Settings → Sync with the iPhone PED). While the crew have the con and the iPad is closed, the phone estimates where the ship is; anything that happens aboard shows when the iPad is opened again.

## 16. Sound

All sounds are made by the game, in a 16-bit style. Sliders in **PED → Settings → Sound**: Master, Ship, Ambient, Communicator, Notices, Interface, Effects.

- **Heard everywhere:** hull hits, groans below 400 m, diving and surfacing, docking clamps, alarms, the drive spooling up and down, the emergency blow, active sonar pings, doors, the communicator.
- **Only on the bridge:** new and lost contacts, the depth warning, incoming message teletype.
- **Only in the launch bay:** the hatches, flooding and pumping, launches.
- **Only in engineering:** the engine room.
- **Water past the hull** grows louder with speed (Ship slider).

The **sound board** (PED → Settings → Sound) plays every sound and lets you rename them.

## Appendix B: Glossary
- **AIS:** ships' automatic identification broadcast, heard on VHF.
- **AO:** Area of Operations.
- **ARIA:** the ship's AI and Executive Officer.
- **DSP:** "display": put the selected desk page on the main screen.
- **DSV:** deep-submergence vessel.
- **Fix:** a contact's position from two crossing bearings.
- **LPI:** low probability of intercept: the quiet acoustic link.
- **NOE:** "nap of the earth": hug the floor.
- **O.E.R.A.:** the Oceanic Exploration and Research Alliance.
- **PED:** personal electronic device: your tablet.
- **SOFAR / deep sound channel:** a layer of the ocean (here 600 to 1200 m) where sound travels very far.
- **WSKR:** the ship's small autonomous sensor craft.
